# 58-note player rolls: identifications

The 58 Note Player tracks have no names on disk. This file records what each
track has been identified as, how sure that is, and why, so the list can grow
disk by disk. Each `T` track is one roll; the disk folders are just floppies
(the `-c`, `-c2`, `-10-2`, `-12-2` folders are re-dumps of the same floppy).

Method: the notes of each track were pulled out with `devfs.py`, then the key
changes, beat, and the top line of the opening and main themes were read off
and compared with candidate pieces (scores where one could be found). The
top line a roll plays is not always the tune, so a match by ear beats a match
on paper. Confidence:

- **Confirmed**: checked by ear.
- **Strong**: the music is unmistakable, or matches a published description closely.
- **Probable**: several independent clues agree; not checked note for note.
- **Guess**: rests mostly on the track's position in the catalogue order (below).

## The rolls follow Aeolian's catalogue order

The 58-note format is the one used by the Aeolian Orchestrelle and the early
Aeolian residence pipe organs, and the player definitions drove the same kind
of organ. Aeolian's 1919 guide to its organ-roll library,
[*The Aeolian Pipe-Organ and Its Music*](https://archive.org/details/aeolianpipeorga01compgoog),
lists the rolls **by composer, then by title**, with a roll number for each.
The end of `58-note-01` follows its sequence exactly:

| Aeolian 1919 order | Roll no. | 58-note-01 |
|---|---:|---|
| Auber, *The Crown Diamonds* — Overture | 879 | T25? |
| Auber, *Fra Diavolo* — Overture | 867 | T27 |
| Auber, *Masaniello* — Overture | 699 | T28? |
| Auber, *Zanetta* — Overture | 405 | T29? |
| Aviles, *La Media Noche* | 707 | T30? |
| Bach, Fugue in G minor ("the greater") | 182 | T31 |

So the alphabetical order of the whole set is most likely **by composer**.
That also fits the other two tracks studied so far: Offenbach's Barcarolle sits
43% of the way through the 470 tracks, and `58-note-15` T4, a 6/8 march, sits
at 86% (composers S–W, e.g. Sousa). The roll numbers above are from the 1919
pipe-organ series; the 58-note rolls may have been numbered differently.

`58-note-01` does not keep to the order everywhere. T3 and T11 are earlier takes
of the Bach that belongs at T31, and T4 is Grieg. Those may be tests or retakes
recorded before the series proper.

## 58-note-02

If the composer order holds, this disk carries on from the end of disk 01 (Bach)
through the early B composers. In Aeolian's 1919 catalogue the next names are
Bach (more organ works), Bache, Bachmann, Bairstow, Balfe, Bargiel, Batiste and
Beethoven (songs, sonatas, the Leonore Overture and movements of Symphonies 1,
2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9), then Bendix, Bellini, Berger, Berlioz, Best and Bizet.
Disk 02's many long tracks (6–12 minutes) fit a run of Beethoven symphony
movements and overtures. Only one track matched a catalogue description
closely enough to name it.

## 58-note-03

Disk 03 is mostly long tracks (9–12 minutes) and, from what could be
identified, sits in the middle of Beethoven, after the run that seemed to
start at the end of disk 02. Within Beethoven the rolls are not in the
1919 catalogue's title order (the sonatas come after the overture here), so
the 58-note catalogue evidently ordered or titled them differently. The image
is byte-identical to `cmm/cmm2`; `cmm/cmm1` is a damaged copy of the same floppy.
